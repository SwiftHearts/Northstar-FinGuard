# Investment Policy Manual

**Document ID:** NWP-IPM-01
**Firm:** Northstar Wealth Partners ("Northstar" or "the Firm")
**Effective Date:** January 1, 2026
**Version:** 3.2
**Owner:** Chief Investment Officer

## 1. Purpose and Scope

This Investment Policy Manual governs how Northstar Wealth Partners constructs, manages, and monitors client portfolios across all advisory programs. It applies to every investment adviser representative (IAR), portfolio manager, and research analyst employed by or contracted to the Firm. Any marketing, sales, or client communication that describes Northstar's investment approach must be consistent with the policies stated here.

## 2. Investment Philosophy

Northstar follows a strategic, evidence-based asset allocation approach grounded in modern portfolio theory. The Firm does not engage in speculative short-term trading on behalf of client accounts and does not claim to consistently "beat the market" through market timing. Portfolio construction is driven by:

- Diversification across asset classes, geographies, and sectors
- Long-term strategic asset allocation informed by client risk tolerance and time horizon
- Periodic tactical adjustments within pre-approved ranges, documented in the Investment Committee minutes
- Low-cost implementation, preferring index and factor-based vehicles where appropriate

## 3. Risk Tolerance Categories

Every client is assigned one of five risk tolerance categories following the completion of the Northstar Risk Profile Questionnaire (Form NWP-RPQ):

| Category | Target Equity Range | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Conservative | 0–25% | Capital preservation priority; minimal volatility tolerance |
| Moderately Conservative | 20–40% | Income-oriented with limited growth exposure |
| Balanced | 40–65% | Growth and income blend |
| Moderately Aggressive | 60–85% | Growth priority; can tolerate meaningful drawdowns |
| Aggressive | 80–100% | Maximum long-term growth; high volatility tolerance |

IARs may not place a client into a risk category more aggressive than their documented questionnaire result without a signed client override acknowledgment (Form NWP-RPQ-OVERRIDE) on file.

## 4. Permitted and Restricted Investments

**Permitted without additional approval:** publicly traded equities and ETFs, investment-grade fixed income, mutual funds on the Northstar Approved List, cash and cash equivalents.

**Requires Investment Committee pre-approval:** alternative investments (private equity, hedge fund structures, non-traded REITs), leveraged or inverse ETFs, individual options strategies, and any structured product.

**Restricted for all client accounts:** uncovered options writing, margin-based leverage exceeding 1.5x for retail accounts, and any cryptocurrency asset not custodied through a Firm-approved qualified custodian.

## 5. Rebalancing Policy

Discretionary accounts are rebalanced to target allocation when any asset class drifts more than 5 percentage points from its target weight, or at minimum on a semi-annual calendar review, whichever occurs first. Rebalancing decisions and rationale must be logged in the portfolio management system.

## 6. Due Diligence on Investment Products

No mutual fund, ETF, or third-party strategy may be added to the Northstar Approved List without a completed due diligence file, including expense ratio analysis, historical performance review (net of fees), manager tenure, and a documented comparison against at least two peer alternatives. Due diligence files are refreshed annually.

## 7. Prohibited Claims About Investment Performance

Consistent with the Marketing Compliance Guidelines (NWP-MKT-03), no communication may describe Northstar's investment approach using language implying guaranteed outcomes, guaranteed protection against loss, or the ability to consistently outperform a stated benchmark. All performance results referenced in any material must be net of fees, cover a period of no less than one year, and comply with the disclosure requirements in Section 5 of NWP-MKT-03.

## 8. Amendments

This manual is reviewed at least annually by the Investment Committee and the Chief Compliance Officer. Interim amendments require sign-off from both the CIO and CCO before taking effect.
